The Dungeons
============

Angband has one dungeon, and depth is the only thing that tells its fortieth
level from its fourth.  Here there are thirteen, and they are places.


A dungeon has a bottom
----------------------

Each dungeon covers a range of depths, and it ends at the bottom of that
range.  When you reach its deepest level there is no way further down from
it.  To go deeper you must leave, cross the world, and find a dungeon that
reaches deeper than the one you are in.

That is the point of the world.  It is also why the ranges overlap: there is
always more than one way onward, and you are never left with nowhere to go.

The staircase in the middle of your home village leads into the shallowest
dungeon there is, so you can start going down on the first turn without
finding anything first.


Each remembers its own depth
----------------------------

Word of recall takes you back to how far down you had got in the dungeon you
were last in -- not to the deepest level you have ever reached anywhere.
That level might be in a dungeon on the far side of the world, and might be
deeper than the dungeon you are standing on goes.


Finding one
-----------

A dungeon's mouth is drawn as a '>' out in the open country, like any
staircase.  Most of them open on or near a road, so following roads is how
you find them; they show on the world map ('M') once you have been near them.

Where a dungeon opens follows from the country around it.  The Caverns of
Kolvir open high in settled land; the Abyss opens in empty lowland.

Each has a floor of its own underfoot, and its own preference in how its
levels are laid out, so the caverns under Kolvir are caverns and the city in
the sky is a maze.


What lives there
----------------

Each dungeon is home to some kinds of creature and not others, and what you
meet is mostly what lives there.  The Caverns of Kolvir are full of trolls
and giants; Tir-na Nog'th has vortices, wraiths and ghosts; Arden is animals
and trees.  Something from elsewhere turns up now and then -- a dungeon is
not sealed -- but it is the exception.

This is worth knowing before you dive.  A dungeon whose inhabitants you are
badly equipped for is more dangerous than its depth suggests, and one you are
well equipped for is less.  Fire is a poor idea in Arden.


What is found there
-------------------

Each dungeon runs to its own kind of treasure, in four rough proportions:
coin and jewellery, arms and armour, wands and potions and scrolls, and
useful gear.

Rebma is rich and Garnath is not.  The Grove of the Unicorn and Tir-na
Nog'th turn up three times the usual magic and almost nothing to fight
with.  The Caverns of Kolvir, being a mine, turn up tools.

Nothing is impossible anywhere -- a sword can still be found in the Grove.
It is a matter of proportion, and of where it is worth going to look.
