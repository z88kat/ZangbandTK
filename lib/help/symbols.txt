Symbols On Your Map
===================

Symbols on your map can be broken down into three categories: Features of
the world such as walls, floor, doors, traps, and the open country between
the towns; Objects which can be picked up such as treasure, weapons,
magical devices, etc; and creatures which may or may not move about, but
are mostly harmful to your character's well being.

Some symbols are used to represent more than one type of entity, and some
symbols are used to represent entities in more than one category. The "@"
symbol (by default) is used to represent the character.

It will not be necessary to remember all of the symbols and their meanings.
The "slash" command ('/') will identify any character appearing on your
map (see 'commands.txt').

Note that you can use a "user pref file" to change any of these symbols to
something you are more comfortable with.



Features that do not block line of sight
----------------------------------------

.  A floor space                 1  Entrance to General Store
.  A trap (hidden)               2  Entrance to Armoury
^  A trap (known)                3  Entrance to Weapon Smith
;  A glyph of warding            4  Entrance to Bookseller
'  An open door                  5  Entrance to Alchemy Shop
'  A broken door                 6  Entrance to Magic Shop
<  A staircase up                7  Entrance to the Black Market
>  A staircase down              8  Entrance to your Home
#  A pool of lava

Not every town holds all eight.  A village keeps three or four trades, a
town five or six, a city or a great city more; which ones it keeps depends
on the country around it.  Your home village is the smallest of them, and
has neither an armoury nor a weapon smith -- those are in the next town
along.


Features that block line of sight
---------------------------------

#  A secret door                 #  A wall
+  A closed door                 %  A mineral vein
+  A locked door                 *  A mineral vein with treasure
:  A pile of rubble              :  A pile of passable rubble


Out of doors
------------

Most of these are told apart by colour rather than by symbol, so the "slash"
command ('/') is worth using freely until you know them.  Several share a
symbol with something you will already know from below ground -- a stand of
trees is drawn like a mineral vein, and a mountainside like a pile of
rubble.

.  Open grassland (green)      %  A stand of trees (light green)
.  Bare earth (brown)          :  Mountainside, impassable (brown)
.  Sand, by the water          ~  Shallow water; wadeable (light blue)
.  Mud; heavy going            ~  Deep water and open sea (blue)
.  A made road (white)         >  The mouth of a dungeon (violet)


The road is worth following.  Roads run between the towns and out to every
dungeon mouth, so if you are lost in open country, find one and walk along
it -- it goes somewhere.  A road is three grids wide and its corners are
squared off, so where it turns it still looks like a road; if it seems to
stop, look around before believing it.  Deep water can be waded into, but
not while heavily laden, and the sea at the edge of the world is the edge of
the world.

A '>' out in the open is the mouth of one of the world's dungeons rather than
an ordinary staircase.  Each leads somewhere different and only so deep; see
'dungeons.txt'.

The status line says "Day" or "Night" while you are above ground.  It matters:
daylight is what reveals the country, and at night you see as far as your lamp
and no further, which looks like an empty map until the sun comes up.

Night lasts 5,000 turns, out of a 10,000-turn day; caught out at random, you
have about 2,500 to wait.  That is 5,000 turns at normal speed -- the world
keeps time whatever your speed, so being fast means fewer of your own moves
spent in the dark rather than a shorter night.  Time passes underground too,
so a long trip down can put you back on the surface after dark.  Resting it
out is 5,000 turns of resting; the inn is the answer, and it sells a bed only
once it is dark.

A '+' in a town is a service: something with a person behind the door rather
than goods on a shelf.  They are told apart by colour, and the "slash" command
('/') will name one.

  green   healer      binds wounds, cures ailments, restores drained stats
                          and levels lost to life-draining attacks
  brown   inn         a bed until morning, which only helps at night
  red     magesmith   puts magic on a weapon or a suit of armour
  blue    recharger   puts charges back in a wand or a staff

A town has a healer, an inn and a magetower; a city adds the magesmith and the
recharger.  A town that has fallen has none of them.  All of them charge.

Shops come at a standard as well as a trade, and it is written above the door:
a plain "Weapon Smiths", or an "Advanced", "Expert" or "Arcane" one.  A better
shop deals in deeper goods, puts better magic on them, and keeps a
fuller shelf.
Seven in ten are plain and one in fifty is arcane, so the good ones are worth
travelling for.  The black market has no standard -- it is already the top of
every ladder -- and your home village is always plain.

A '9' in a town is a magetower.  Walk in and the mages will carry you to a
town you have stood in or a dungeon mouth you have seen, for a fee that rises
with the distance.  Anything above a village has one, unless it has fallen --
and your own village always does.  You must be standing in a tower to use one.

Every town has a name, and the status line shows it while you are inside the
walls -- where Angband showed "Town".  Outside one it says "wilderness",
which is what depth zero mostly is.  A name from the lawless list, like
Sawall or Hendrake, is a place that has fallen: not somewhere to shop.

Not every town has people in it.  Most do; about one in four has been emptied
and taken back by animals, one in ten is held by monsters, and one in twenty
stands empty with its shops still in it.  Law decides it: a town in country
nobody polices has more often than not fallen, and a large town holds out
longer than a hamlet.  Your own village always has people in it.

Towns stand behind walls, with gates no more than two grids wide.  A gate is
a closed door ('+') in the wall; open it to pass through, and it will swing
shut behind you after a while.


Objects
-------

!  A potion (or flask)           /  A pole-arm
?  A scroll (or book)            |  An edged weapon
,  A mushroom (or food)          \  A hafted weapon
-  A wand or rod                 }  A sling, bow, or x-bow
_  A staff                       {  A shot, arrow, or bolt
=  A ring                        (  Soft armour
"  An amulet                     [  Hard armour
$  Gold or gems                  ]  Misc. armour
~  Lights, Tools, Chests, etc    )  A shield
&  Multiple items
