Towns and Services
==================

Angband has one town and it is a shop counter.  Here there are a dozen or so
places, they differ from one another, and some of them have gone bad.


What stands in a town
---------------------

Shops are the letters and numbers you know from Angband.  A '+' is a service:
something with a person behind the door rather than goods on a shelf.  They
are told apart by colour, and the "slash" command ('/') will name one.

  green   healer      binds wounds, cures ailments, restores drained stats
                          and levels lost to life-draining attacks
  brown   inn         a bed until morning, and sometimes a dream
  red     magesmith   puts magic on a weapon (to hit and to wound)
                          or on a suit of armour; it asks which
  blue    recharger   puts charges back in a wand or a staff

A '9' is a magetower.  Walk in and the mages will carry you to a town you have
stood in or a dungeon mouth you have seen, for a fee that rises with the
distance.  You must be standing in a tower to use one.

A town has a healer, an inn and a magetower; a city adds the magesmith and the
recharger.  A town that has fallen has none of them.  Anything above a village
has a magetower, and your own village always does.  All of them charge.


Work
----

Some buildings hand out work.  The inn is the one that does for now: walk in
and you may be offered a job before you are offered a bed -- kill so many of
a creature, come back, and be paid for it.

You can carry a few jobs at once.  Press 'J' for the list of what you have
taken on and how far along each one is.  Walk back into any building that
hires and it will tell you, or pay you if you are done.

Not all work is killing.  You may be asked to carry word to another town,
which is finished by arriving; or to go and look at a place nobody here has
been, which is the only work that gives you a piece of the world rather than
asking for one back.

Not every town is hiring.  A village keeps no inn, and a town that has fallen
keeps no services at all, so work is one more thing worth travelling for.


A night at the inn
------------------

The inn sells you a bed until morning, and only after dark -- by day the
innkeeper will tell you to travel while it is light.  It is the answer to a
night in the wilderness: daylight is what reveals the country, night lasts
5,000 turns, and resting that out is 5,000 turns of resting.

But a bed is not only a way of skipping the night.  Some nights you dream.

A TRUE DREAM shows you a place.  The nearest town or dungeon mouth you have
not yet found appears on the world map, and you wake knowing its name and
where it stands.  This is the reason to buy a bed on a night you could have
walked through.

It shows you where the place is.  It does not take you there.  The magetower
carries you only to places you have actually stood in, and a dream is not a
visit -- so you still have to make the walk.  You now know which way, which
is the hard part.

A DARK DREAM sets something hunting you through your sleep.  It will be
something you have met: whatever you have seen and remember, leaning towards
the worst of it.  Hold your nerve and you merely remember the thing in the
morning.  Fail, and you wake frightened or confused, and it wears off.  A
character who has not met anything memorable yet has nothing to dream about.

Which you get depends on the law of the town you sleep in.  A well-ordered
city is a good place to sleep and a frontier town is not:

  a town at the edge of what is governed   true 1 night in 11, dark 1 in 4
  a well-ordered city                      true 1 night in 4,  dark almost
                                                                   never

Most nights, in either, are just a night.  And a town that has fallen has no
inn at all, so the worst country never gets the chance to give you the worst
dreams.


How good a shop is
------------------

Shops come at a standard as well as a trade, and it is written above the door:
a plain "Weapon Smiths", or an "Advanced", "Expert" or "Arcane" one.  A better
shop deals in deeper goods, puts better magic on them, and keeps a fuller
shelf.  Seven in ten are plain and one in fifty is arcane, so the good ones
are worth travelling for.

The black market has no standard -- it is already the top of every ladder --
and your home village is always plain.

A trade's shelves are restocked when you take your custom to a different town.
Walking out of a shop and back in does not change what is in it.


Your house
----------

Every town has a home, and there is only one house: whichever town you open it
in, you see what you left there.  A shop in another town is another shop and
is stocked afresh; your house is not another house.


Which shops a place keeps
-------------------------

Not every place carries all eight.  How many it keeps follows from its size;
which ones it keeps follows from the country around it.  The armoury and the
weaponsmith follow people and order, the bookseller gathers where it is safe
to gather, the magic shop wants both people and law, and the black market
keeps out of the light -- it appears where law is thin and people are many,
which is rare and worth the walk.

Your own village is the exception.  Its shops are fixed rather than drawn: a
general store, an alchemist, a bookseller and your home.  It has no armoury
and no weaponsmith; those are in the next town along.


Names, and towns that have gone bad
-----------------------------------

Every town has a name, and the status line shows it while you are inside the
walls -- where Angband showed "Town".  Outside one it says "wilderness", which
is what depth zero mostly is.  A name from the lawless list, like Sawall or
Hendrake, is a place that has fallen: not somewhere to shop.

The sidebar says which, under the place's name: "village", "town", "city" or
"great city", and in red "city, taken" or "town, wild" or "village, empty" for
one that has fallen.  Read it before you go looking for an inn.

Not every town has people in it.  Most do; about one in four has been emptied
and taken back by animals, one in ten is held by monsters, and one in twenty
stands empty with its shops still in it.  Law decides it: a town in country
nobody polices has more often than not fallen, and a large town holds out
longer than a hamlet.  Your own village always has people in it.

A town that has been taken still trades.  Its shops are doors in walls rather
than shopkeepers, so you can walk in past whatever is holding the place -- but
it keeps no services, and whatever is holding it is still there.


Walls and gates
---------------

Towns stand behind walls, with gates no more than two grids wide.  A gate is a
closed door ('+') in the wall; open it to pass through, and it will swing shut
behind you after a while.
