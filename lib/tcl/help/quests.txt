T	h2	Where work comes from
T	h2	\n
T	p0	From a building. Walk into an inn and the innkeeper asks what you’re after: work, or a bed for the night. Ask about work and you’ll be offered a job if there is one, paid for one you have finished, or asked after one you still owe.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Not in the village you start in, though. The starting village is given one service and only one, a magetower, so that the travel network has a node you can leave from on your first day. It has no inn, so you cannot find work at home. The first work you are offered will be somewhere you’ve walked to.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Quest-giving is a property that a building has. There’s no quest-giver’s hut and there never will be. At the moment the inn has the property, and it could as easily be the magetower commissioning a retrieval or a temple sending you on a pilgrimage. That way a new source of work costs a line rather than a new building, a new door and a new kind of terrain.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	The inn has it because it is where people who have been somewhere else are sitting. And because a town that has fallen keeps no services at all, the work dries up exactly where you’d expect it to, without any rule needing to say so. Every inn is hiring; a village has no inn, and nor does a town that has fallen.

T	h2	What the game is won by
T	h2	\n
T	p0	Two quests, and nobody offers them to you: Oberon, at the ninety-ninth level of the Courts of Chaos, and then the Serpent of Chaos at the hundredth.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Angband ends at Sauron and Morgoth. Zangband replaced them with Oberon and the Serpent, and that was already the right choice for this game, because the Serpent is Zelazny’s. The bestiary’s own description of it has been sitting in the imported data all along: 
T	p0 em	“The Unicorn of Order fought with Serpent and stole one of its eyes, known as the Jewel of Judgement. With the Jewel, Dworkin drew the Pattern and thus gave birth to the infinite worlds of shadow.”
T	p0	 The 
L	p0 link	2	monsters	Unicorn
T	p0	 is in this game, and the Serpent is the other half of her.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	A quest names its dungeon as well as its depth, which Angband never needed to do: it has one dungeon, so a depth is a place. Here the Courts of Chaos run from 75 to 110 and the Abyss from 90 to 127, so depth 100 is two different places, and without naming the dungeon the ending could be reached in the wrong one.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	All six of Zangband’s kinds of work are in the game. Which one you are offered depends on what the world can supply: an errand to another town needs another town, a job down a particular dungeon needs one you’ve found, and there’s no sense sending you to look at a place you’re standing in. Every kind falls back to a bounty, which needs nothing but a bestiary.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	The six aren’t offered equally often, though, so don’t conclude that the world only wants vermin killed. The roll picks a place to start in a fixed order (finding a place, then a delivery, then a dungeon job, then a fetch) and works down from wherever it landed, taking the first kind the world can supply. So a fetch is reachable from every starting point and finding a place from only one, and the four are offered in that order of likelihood. One roll in five starts past the end of the list and offers none of them. A killing in the open is then tried one time in three. Everything that falls through all of it becomes a bounty.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Expect more bounties than anything else, and most of all early on, when you have found one dungeon and heard of one town and most of the list has nothing to work with.
