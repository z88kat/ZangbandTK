T	h2	Three new weapon properties
T	h2	\n
T	p0	These are the mechanics most likely to change how you fight, and all three can appear on artifacts or on ordinary ego items.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	Vampiric.
T	p0	 Drains life from living creatures to heal you, by the damage each blow deals. A killing blow drains nothing — the life has gone with the monster, as it had in Zangband — so it is the blows that do not kill that feed you. Undead and other non-living creatures give you nothing. Found on artifacts and on the 
T	p0 code	(Vampiric)
T	p0	 ego.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	Vorpal.
T	p0	 One blow in six cuts far deeper than it should, doubling that blow’s damage. It multiplies the weapon’s own contribution rather than your damage bonuses, so it favours a large weapon over a heavily enchanted small one. Found on artifacts and on weapons 
T	p0 code	of Sharpness
T	p0	.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	Chaotic.
T	p0	 One blow in seven discharges an unpredictable effect into whatever you hit — confusion, terror, slowing, stunning, or flinging the target out of reach entirely. That last one is a genuine drawback and is meant to be: a chaotic weapon that only ever helped you would not be chaotic. Found on artifacts and on the 
T	p0 code	(Chaotic)
T	p0	 ego.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	All three announce themselves in an item’s description, so you will know a weapon has them once you have identified it.

T	h2	The Ring of Fate
T	h2	\n
T	p0	It sounds like a lucky charm. It is not, quite.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	What it actually does is sharpen every critical hit in the game by half again — 
T	p0 strong	including the ones landed on you
T	p0	. And roughly one monster in thirteen generated while you wear it comes from deeper than the level should produce, sometimes forty levels deeper.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	So it is a good ring for a character who kills things quickly and a very bad one for a character who does not, and it will eventually introduce you to something you were not ready for. Zangband’s own source comments the third of its four effects with “Luck isn’t always good for you…”, which is the whole item in six words.
T	p0	\n
M	the-ancient-and-foul-curse
T	h2	The Ancient and Foul Curse
T	h2	\n
T	p0	Some equipment carries the Curse of Topi Ylinen, and you will know it: the item describes itself as ancient and foul.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	What makes it feared is not any single outcome but its shape. Roughly once every hundred turns, a cursed item stirs and one misfortune befalls you — monsters woken, something summoned, experience drained, paralysis, a statistic lost, or your memory torn away. But each of those has a chance of 
T	p0 em	falling through
T	p0	 to the next, and the next after that, so a woken monster can escalate into a summoning, into paralysis, into total amnesia, in a single visitation. When it finishes, there is a one-in-three chance it simply begins again.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Free action does not make you immune to the paralysis. It gives you a saving throw, and shortens the paralysis if you fail.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Sometimes the curse stirs and nothing happens at all.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	It is worth carrying cursed equipment only if what it gives you is worth this. Often it is.
T	p0	\n
M	activations
T	h2	Activations
T	h2	\n
T	p0	Zangband scripted its artifact activations in a scripting language ZangbandTK does not carry. Each has been matched to the nearest Angband activation instead — an artifact that threw a fireball still throws a fireball, and its recharge time is preserved, but the effect is Angband’s version rather than a faithful reproduction of Zangband’s.

T	h2	The lotus
T	h2	\n
T	p0	There is a mushroom that takes your memory.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	It looks like every other mushroom you have not identified — some colour and a comma on the floor — and eating it says only that you feel a little dizzy. Five turns later you forget everything you know:
T	p0	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	the map of the level you are standing on;
T	li1	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	the world map, and every town and dungeon mouth you had found on it;
T	li1	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	everything you had learned about every monster;
T	li1	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	what every flavoured thing is, so your potions go back to being coloured liquids and have to be drunk to find out what they are;
T	li1	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	and every spell you had learned, which must be studied again from the book.
T	li1	\n
T	p0	It takes nothing you cannot get back. No experience, no levels, no items. But none of it comes back quickly, so the price of eating a strange mushroom is measured in hours of play rather than in a dead character — which is the point. A consumable that could end a run outright is one nobody eats twice.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	One thing survives: you always know where home is.
T	p0	 The village you started in stays on the map, along with the country immediately around it, so there is always one place you can name and one direction worth walking. That is not mercy — the magetower only carries you to places you have found, so a character who had forgotten every place including their own would have a blank map, no travel and nothing to aim at.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Which is also where the idea comes from. The first of Zelazny’s Amber novels opens on a man who has lost his memory and knows only that there is a place called Amber, and that he is of it.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	The five turns are deliberate.
T	p0	 Something that took your memory the moment you swallowed it would be an ordinary bad mushroom. Five turns is long enough to work out what you have done and not long enough to do anything about it.

M	seven-more-from-zangband
T	h2	Seven more from Zangband
T	h2	\n
T	p0 strong	Returning.
T	p0	 A thrown weapon comes back to your hand, nineteen throws in twenty. The twentieth is what keeps it a weapon you can lose.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	Luck.
T	p0	 Improves your saving throw. Zangband wrote this as a flat value rather than a bonus, which made a lucky novice better at shrugging things off than an unlucky veteran; here it is the bonus the name promises.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	A ghoul’s touch.
T	p0	 What you strike falls asleep — but only if you strike it bare-handed. Gloves of this kind are for someone who fights with their hands, and putting a weapon in them turns the property off entirely.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	Easily enchanted.
T	p0	 Takes twice as many enchantment attempts, and an artifact so made does not get its usual chance to resist being enchanted at all.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	Weird luck.
T	p0	 The Ring of Fate’s property, described above. Both edges of it — and a third thing besides: once in about seven levels gained, something in the Courts of Chaos notices you and hands down a reward. It will not be a Lord of your own, and one that was never yours is gentler than one that is — a passing interest rather than a claim.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	Magic-powered criticals.
T	p0	 A weapon that spends your mana to land critical hits, and to make the ones it lands worse — one to three points each time. The 
T	p0 strong	Psiblade
T	p0	 carries it. With an empty mana pool it does nothing at all, which makes it a warrior’s weapon only in the hands of a warrior who casts.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	An anti-magic shell.
T	p0	 You cannot cast. Not a reduced chance, not a higher cost: the spell simply does not happen. Studying and browsing still work, so you can keep learning spells you are unable to use.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	A chaos patron.
T	p0	 Carried by the 
T	p0 code	(Chaotic)
T	p0	 ego. A Lord of the Courts takes an interest in whoever holds it, and every level you gain from then on brings a reward — the same ladder a Chaos-Warrior climbs, from the genuinely generous down to the actively hostile. Wearing one is not the same as being sworn to one, and the difference runs the way you would not guess: a Lord with no claim on you is 
T	p0 em	less
T	p0	 likely to reach for the bottom of the ladder, not more. It is glancing over rather than keeping accounts, and the borrowed reward is a smaller thing in both directions.

M	not-yet-implemented
T	h2	Not yet implemented
T	h2	\n
T	p0	One Zangband object property is recorded but inactive: passing through walls. Angband has no form of player wall-movement to hang it on — not a spell, not a temporary state, nothing — so carrying it would mean building wall-phasing for the player rather than reading a flag. Items with it work in every other respect.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Two more object properties are recorded and inactive, and both are the same kind of case: each cancels a penalty this game does not impose. 
T	p0 code	WILD_SHOT
T	p0	 ignores the cover a monster gets from standing in trees, and Angband has no terrain term in its combat at all. 
T	p0 code	WILD_WALK
T	p0	 removes the extra effort of climbing a mountain and the poison of wading a swamp — but here a mountainside is a wall rather than slow ground, and there is no swamp terrain. Building either flag alone would do nothing; building what they cancel is a change to how the wilderness plays rather than a conversion of a flag. One ego, 
T	p0 code	of the Wild
T	p0	, waits with them: every property it had was 
T	p0 code	WILD_WALK
T	p0	, so importing it would have put a pair of boots in the game that granted nothing at all.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Thirteen of Zangband’s object kinds are likewise recorded but not imported, each for want of a mechanism rather than out of preference:
