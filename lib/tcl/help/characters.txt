T	h2	\n

M	the-monk-and-martial-arts
T	h2	The Monk, and martial arts
T	h2	\n
T	p0	The first of Zangband’s five classes, and the one that needed a mechanism Angband does not have.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	In Angband, fighting bare-handed is a punishment: one point of damage a blow, criticals skipped outright. A Monk turns that on its head. It carries no weapon because it does not need one, and unarmed it climbs a ladder of seventeen techniques — a punch at level 1 up to a crushing blow at 48 — throwing two strikes a turn at the start and 
T	p0 strong	eight
T	p0	 by the end, which is more than a Warrior gets out of any weapon in the game.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Which technique lands is decided fresh on every swing, so a Grand Master usually — but never reliably — throws something from the top of the ladder. Some strikes stun; a knee doubles a male opponent up; a kick to the ankle leaves anything that walks limping.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	And it is the one class in the game that is 
T	p0 strong	punished for wearing armour
T	p0	. Over the weight limit and the strikes halve and every bonus withdraws; under it, each empty armour slot is worth armour class in its own right. See 
L	p0 link	4	birth#martial-arts	Martial arts
T	p0	.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Two more things hang off that limit. A Monk 
T	p0 strong	speeds up
T	p0	 from level 10, by one point for every ten levels, and 
T	p0 strong	cannot be held
T	p0	 from level 25 — and both of them go away the moment it is carrying too much armour, along with the blows and the armour class. Nothing else in the game gates an intrinsic on what you are wearing. It is the clearest statement of what the class is: a Monk’s advantages are all things that stop being true when it puts something on.

T	h2	The Mindcrafter, and psionics
T	h2	\n
T	p0	The second of Zangband’s classes, and the opposite kind of thing to the Monk.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	A Mindcrafter has no spellbooks and will never find any. Its craft is twelve powers that arrive as it levels — and they are deliberately 
T	p0 strong	not a realm of magic
T	p0	: nothing to study, nothing to choose at birth, nothing that can be taken away. That distinction is the whole requirement, not a detail of how it was built.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Most of the twelve grow into something else as the character does, which is worth knowing because it is unlike anything in Angband. 
T	p0 em	See what is coming
T	p0	 spots monsters at level 2, finds traps and doors at 5, sees invisibility at 15, maps the level at 20, grants telepathy from 25, detects everything at 30 and lights the whole level at 45 — one power, one key, for a whole career.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	And psionic force is a new kind of damage.
T	p0	 Every damaging type Angband has is an element, resisted with a flag or with armour. This one asks whether there is a mind there to hurt: a golem, a mould or an animated weapon is completely unaffected however hard it is hit, and a strange or feeble mind takes a third. Against anything else, nothing resists it. See

T	p0	.

M	the-chaos-warrior-and-who-owns-it
T	h2	The Chaos-Warrior, and who owns it
T	h2	\n
T	p0	The third of Zangband’s classes, and the only one in the game that belongs to somebody.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	At birth a Chaos-Warrior is sworn to one of nine Lords of the Courts of Chaos. It does not choose which, it cannot leave, and every time it gains a level its Lord looks up and decides how it feels about the servant it has. Usually that is a gift — a healing, a raised stat, experience, an object out of nothing. Sometimes it is a cursed weapon, a ruined stat, monsters dropped on top of you, or the level destroyed around you. The class fights nearly as well as a Warrior, and the relationship 
T	p0 em	is
T	p0	 the class.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	It also casts.
T	p0	 A Chaos-Warrior learns Chaos magic from level 2 — all thirty-two spells, and it is the only class in the game with exactly one realm and no choice about it. Zangband is blunt on the point: they are “not interested in any other form of magic”. Until the Chaos realm existed there was nothing to give them and the class shipped unable to hold spell points at all.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	And thirteen is an unlucky level.
T	p0	 The odds of the cruel end of the repertoire are normally about one in six; on reaching level 13 they are one in two. Every other thirteenth level is one in three, and every fourteenth is one in twelve — the safest a Chaos-Warrior ever gets. It is nowhere in the interface and a player can only learn it by living through it.
T	p0	\n
T	p0 strong	The nine Lords are Zelazny’s.
T	p0	 This is the clearest case in the game of the drift this project exists to undo. Zangband’s sixteen patrons were Moorcock’s Elric gods — Slortar, Arioch, Xiombarg, Mabelode — with Khorne, Slaanesh, Nurgle and Tzeentch from Warhammer among them, and not one had any connection to Amber. They are replaced by Swayvill the King of Chaos, Suhuy who keeps the Logrus, Mandor, Dara, Gramble, Jurt, Despil, Borel and Gilva. Four of them are already walking around in the bestiary, so you can be gifted by a Lord you later meet.
T	p0	\n
M	what-every-class-costs
T	h2	What every class costs
T	h2	\n
T	p0	Some classes learn more slowly than others, and in this game that is a real price rather than a formality. A Warrior needs the least experience of anyone to reach a level; a Warrior-Mage needs half again as much.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	The arrangement is Zangband’s and it is not the one most people expect. It is not that casters are expensive — the pure casters are among the 
T	p0 em	cheapest
T	p0	 after the Warrior, because a Mage is already paying in hit points and strength. What costs is having 
T	p0 strong	two things at once
T	p0	. The Warrior-Mage fights and casts, and pays 150 per cent. The Monk fights unarmed and casts, and pays 140. The Paladin and the Chaos-Warrior pay 135. The Priest, who only prays, pays 120.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Ten of the eleven classes Zangband has pay something. Only the Warrior is free, and that is the whole of what a Warrior gets for having no magic at all.
