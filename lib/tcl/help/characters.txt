M	the-monk-and-martial-arts
T	h2	The Monk, and martial arts
T	h2	\n
T	p0	The Monk is the first of Zangband’s five classes, and it needed a mechanism Angband does not have.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	In Angband, fighting bare-handed is a punishment: one point of damage a blow, with criticals skipped outright. A Monk turns that on its head. It carries no weapon because it doesn’t need one. Unarmed, it climbs a ladder of seventeen techniques, from a punch at level 1 up to a crushing blow at 48. It throws two strikes a turn at the start and eight by the end, which is more than a Warrior gets out of any weapon in the game.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Which technique lands is decided fresh on every swing, so a Grand Master usually throws something from the top of the ladder, but never reliably. Some strikes stun, a knee doubles a male opponent up, and a kick to the ankle leaves anything that walks limping.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	It’s also the one class in the game that is punished for wearing armour. Over the weight limit, the strikes halve and every bonus is withdrawn. Under it, each empty armour slot is worth armour class in its own right. See 
L	p0 link	4	birth#martial-arts	Martial arts
T	p0	.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Two more things hang off that limit. A Monk speeds up from level 10, by one point for every ten levels, and cannot be held from level 25. Both go away the moment it’s carrying too much armour, along with the blows and the armour class. Nothing else in the game gates an intrinsic on what you’re wearing. Every one of a Monk’s advantages stops being true when it puts something on.
T	p0	\n

T	h2	The Mindcrafter, and psionics
T	h2	\n
T	p0	The Mindcrafter is the second of Zangband’s classes, and the opposite kind of thing to the Monk.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	A Mindcrafter has no spellbooks and will never find any. Its craft is twelve powers that arrive as it levels, and they aren’t a realm of magic: there’s nothing to study, nothing to choose at birth, and nothing that can be taken away.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Most of the twelve grow into something else as the character does, which is unlike anything in Angband. 
T	p0 em	See what is coming
T	p0	 spots monsters at level 2, finds traps and doors at 5, sees invisibility at 15, maps the level at 20, grants telepathy from 25, detects everything at 30 and lights the whole level at 45. That’s one power on one key for a whole career.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Psionic force is a new kind of damage, too. Every damaging type Angband has is an element, resisted with a flag or with armour. This one asks whether there’s a mind there to hurt. A golem, a mould or an animated weapon is completely unaffected however hard it is hit, and a strange or feeble mind takes a third. Against anything else, nothing resists it. See

M	the-chaos-warrior-and-who-owns-it
T	h2	The Chaos-Warrior, and who owns it
T	h2	\n
T	p0	The Chaos-Warrior is the third of Zangband’s classes, and the only one in the game that belongs to somebody.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	At birth a Chaos-Warrior is sworn to one of nine Lords of the Courts of Chaos. It doesn’t choose which, it can’t leave, and every time it gains a level its Lord looks up and decides how it feels about the servant it has. Usually that’s a gift: a healing, a raised stat, experience, an object out of nothing. Sometimes it’s a cursed weapon, a ruined stat, monsters dropped on top of you, or the level destroyed around you. The class fights nearly as well as a Warrior, but the relationship with its Lord is what the class is about.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	It also casts. A Chaos-Warrior learns Chaos magic from level 2, all thirty-two spells, and like a Druid, a Necromancer or a Blackguard it has exactly one realm and no choice about it. Zangband is blunt on the point: they are “not interested in any other form of magic”. Until the Chaos realm existed there was nothing to give them, and the class shipped unable to hold spell points at all.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Thirteen is an unlucky level. The odds of the cruel end of the repertoire are normally about one in six; on reaching level 13 they’re one in two. Every other thirteenth level is one in three, and every fourteenth is one in twelve, the safest a Chaos-Warrior ever gets. The interface never shows it, and you can only learn it by living through it.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	The nine Lords are Zelazny’s. Zangband’s sixteen patrons were Moorcock’s Elric gods (Slortar, Arioch, Xiombarg, Mabelode) with Khorne, Slaanesh, Nurgle and Tzeentch from Warhammer among them, and not one had any connection to Amber. It’s the clearest case in the game of the drift this project exists to undo. They have been replaced by Swayvill the King of Chaos, Suhuy who keeps the Logrus, Mandor, Dara, Gramble, Jurt, Despil, Borel and Gilva. Four of them are already walking around in the bestiary, so you can be gifted by a Lord you later meet.
T	p0	\n

T	h2	What every class costs
T	h2	\n
T	p0	Some classes learn more slowly than others, and in this game you’ll feel the difference. A Warrior needs the least experience of anyone to reach a level; a Warrior-Mage needs half again as much.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	The arrangement is Zangband’s, and it isn’t the one most people expect. Casters aren’t the expensive ones. The pure casters are among the 
T	p0 em	cheapest
T	p0	 after the Warrior, because a Mage is already paying in hit points and strength. What costs is doing two things at once. The Warrior-Mage fights and casts, and pays 150 per cent. The Monk fights unarmed and casts, and pays 140. The Paladin and the Chaos-Warrior pay 135. The Priest, who only prays, pays 120.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Ten of the eleven classes Zangband has pay something. Only the Warrior is free. That, and the books it can burn (below), are all a Warrior gets for having no magic at all.
T	p0	\n

T	h2	Burning books
T	h2	\n
T	p0	A Warrior or a Paladin can burn a spellbook for experience. Zangband did this in its command for destroying things. Angband 4.2 has no such command, so here it’s a choice in the ignore menu.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Only the rare books count: the two in each realm that are found in the dungeon and never sold. Arcane has none, so an Arcane book never counts. Who gets paid for which depends on the class:
T	p0	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	A Warrior, for any of them.
T	li1	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	A Paladin of Life, for any except Life’s own.
T	li1	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	A Paladin of Death, for Life’s and nobody else’s.
T	li1	\n
T	p0	A book is worth a twentieth of your maximum experience, never more than 10,000 and never less than 1. The first of a realm’s two dungeon books is worth a quarter of that. A Warrior with 200,000 experience gets 2,500 for Book of the Unicorn and 10,000 for Blessings of the Grail, and burning a stack pays for each book in it. No other class gets anything for burning a book, so no other class is offered it.
T	p0	\n

T	p0	 at the bottom of the menu. You’re asked how many if there’s more than one, and then whether you really mean it. It takes a turn.
