T	p0 code	y
T	p0	 on it, then 
T	p0 code	ESC
T	p0	 back to the character you were making.

M	knowing-you-are-in-it
T	h2	Knowing you are in it
T	h2	\n
T	p0	A character playing under nightmare mode has 
T	p0 strong	Nightmare
T	p0	 on the status line, in red, for as long as it lives. It sits beside 
T	p0 code	Cheat
T	p0	 and is there for the same reason: the mode changes sixteen things and every one of them looks, from inside the game, like the ordinary game going badly. A monster with twice the hit points looks like a monster. A sustain that failed looks like a sustain that was never bought. A wall that looks like floor looks like floor.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Zangband didn’t have that marker. It showed the mode in one place only, the character dump, and a dump is a file you write after the character is dead.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	The dump still says it too, as 
T	p0 code	Nightmare Mode: ON
T	p0	, because that’s where characters get compared with each other. A character that died at depth 20 under nightmare did something quite different from one that died at depth 20 without it.
T	p0	\n
M	what-it-changes
T	h2	What it changes
T	h2	\n
T	p0	Every change in this section comes from Zangband’s own code.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Monsters have twice the hit points: everything you meet has double what it would otherwise have. That’s on top of the game’s own lethality scaling, so a monster’s hit points are 
T	p0 code	base × 0.73 × 2
T	p0	.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Every monster in the game also gets a permanent 
T	p0 code	+5
T	p0	 to speed, on top of whatever haste or slow is acting on it. At normal speed that’s the difference between trading blows and being hit twice for every one you land.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Monster recall shows both of these as they’ll be in the fight. In nightmare mode the page you read before fighting something already has the doubled hit points and the extra five points of speed in it, whatever the monster’s record says.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Nothing is asleep when it arrives. Monsters that would normally be found sleeping are awake from the moment the level is made. They can still be put to sleep afterwards; the mode doesn’t stop that, whatever its spoiler says.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Nothing waits for you, either. Ordinarily the game marks certain dangerous monsters so that they give you one free move when they first appear. That courtesy stops a summoned horror acting before you’ve seen it, and under nightmare there isn’t one.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Everything arrives half-started. A monster placed on a level begins with a random amount of energy towards its next turn, and under nightmare it begins with twice as much, so in practice it acts sooner after it appears.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Sleepers are twice as alert. A sleeping monster’s chance of noticing you on any given turn is doubled. Stealth still works, but only half as well.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Sustains fail, and drains stick. A sustain holds twelve times in thirteen instead of always, and when a stat drain does land it’s permanent twelve times in thirteen instead of wearing off. This is probably the cruellest thing in the mode, and nothing in Zangband’s own documentation mentions it. A sustain is a property you buy once and stop thinking about, and one that fails now and then is a different item from one that holds.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	No stairs can be made. Stair creation does nothing, so there’s no scumming for a convenient descent and no escape hatch when a level turns out badly. You leave a floor by the stairs that are on it.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	What lives on a floor isn’t what should live there, and that’s two changes at once. The game’s ordinary rule for putting something out of its depth adds a few levels at most; under nightmare it multiplies, uncapped, so a monster on the fifth floor can be one you’d expect eighty floors down. And the monsters normally pinned to their own depth (the ones the game will never generate early, whatever else it does) aren’t pinned any more. Only quest monsters still are.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Some walls look like floor: a few on every level, and one door in several hundred. They are still walls. You can’t walk through them or see past them, and nothing about how they’re drawn will tell you. You find them by walking into them.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	A bell tolls before midnight, and then something answers it. It rings four times in the last hour, at eleven and at each quarter past, and on the stroke of midnight the Ancient and Foul Curse falls on you wherever you are. The bell is the only warning the mode gives you about anything, so heed it. There’s time to drink something, or to be somewhere else.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Word of Recall occasionally takes you somewhere worse. About one recall in six hundred and sixty-six arrives deeper than you asked: twice as deep in the upper dungeon, half the remaining way down in the lower. It won’t take you past the bottom of the dungeon you’re recalling into, so in a shallow one this does little. In a deep one it can end a character who was going shopping.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	A Golem can be stunned. Ordinarily nothing stuns one, but Zangband made the Golem for learning on and takes that away in a hard game. Nightmare is one of three ways to ask for one; forced descent and closed stores are the others (see 
L	p0 link	1	birth#golem	Golem
T	p0	). For a while this page said the Golem kept it, because the other two options weren’t in this game yet.
T	p0	\n
M	and-four-things-it-does-not-do
T	h2	And four things it does not do
T	h2	\n
T	p0	Everything in the list above is Zangband’s. These four are 
T	p0 em	ours
T	p0	: Zangband’s spoiler describes a long menu of ideas its code never implemented, and these were the four worth building. They’re kept apart from the list above because that list is a port and this one is a design. None of it restores anything.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Breeders fill a level two and a half times over. The cap on how many breeding monsters a level will hold goes up from 100 to 255, so a floor of giant white lice or mice, left alone, gets very much worse than it ordinarily would. Scrolls of Sterilize still work and still stop it.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Your reflection lets twice as many bolts through. A character with reflection ordinarily turns aside nine bolts in ten, and here eight in ten. Monsters that reflect are unaffected: they bounce your bolts back exactly as often as they always did. The property is still worth having, but it isn’t close to a guarantee any more.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	The changes chaos works on you are worse ones. Ten of the mutations are pure penalty (puny, moronic, albino, rotting flesh, a blank face, short legs, arthritis, elemental vulnerability, extra noise and extra fat), and in nightmare mode each is twice as likely to be the one you get. Ordinarily a mutation lands on one of them about one time in eight; here it’s closer to one in five. Two others are just as bad on paper and are left alone, because they do nothing at all. A silly voice and bad luck both lost their mechanism when 4.2 removed charisma and the identify moment, and making a no-op likelier does not make anything nastier.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	The shopkeepers are poor, and they never leave. Each shop is run by whichever of its owners has the smallest purse, and the usual rotation that replaces a shopkeeper every so often doesn’t happen. So the most any shop will pay you for a find is as low as it can be, for the whole game, and waiting for a richer buyer isn’t a plan.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Three more things won’t be built, and one of those is a permanent decision:
T	p0	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	Your score is unaffected. Zangband gives nightmare characters a score bonus, but this game’s scoring has no multiplier of any kind to attach one to, for any birth option. Adding one would mean inventing a system just to port a number.
T	li1	\n
T	p0	The other two are the mode’s two nightmares, which Zangband triggers at the inn and when a sleep attack lands on you:
T	p0	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	Sleeping at the inn is unchanged. This game already dreams there, in a form built from its own parts and keyed on the town’s law instead of Zangband’s sanity system, which this project does not have and is not going to. Making those dreams darker under nightmare would mean inventing a number for a piece of the game you meet once a night at most.
T	li1	\n
T	li1	•\t
T	li1	A sleep attack can’t give you nightmares, because nothing can put you to sleep. There is no player sleep in this game at all. Monsters can paralyse you, but that’s a different thing, and Zangband’s trigger has nowhere to attach. This one is deferred, not refused: if a sleep state ever arrives for some other reason, the nightmare can hang off it.
T	li1	\n
M	a-note-on-the-spoiler
T	h2	A note on the spoiler
T	h2	\n
T	p0	If you find Zangband’s own nightmare documentation, it describes a great deal more than this: doubled monster damage, monsters that never miss, spells that never fail, resurrection, home robbery, and about thirty other things. Most of it was never written. Zangband 2.7.5 checks its nightmare flag in twenty-four places and does nineteen things, and this game is built from those. Anything added beyond them will be described here as ours.
