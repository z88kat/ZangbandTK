T	h2	What reads them
T	h2	\n
T	p0	Two things read them, and Zangband had neither. The first is the Lords of the Courts of Chaos. A Chaos-Warrior’s patron (or whoever has taken an interest in a character carrying the right item) hands down a reward on every level gained, from the generous to the actively hostile. What it reaches for depends on how you’ve lived. A Lord of Chaos isn’t impressed by a well-run life: it recognises Chance and Individualism, and it wearies of Harmony and Temperance. Live chaotically and the worst of the ladder comes up less often.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	The second is the dream at the inn. A night’s sleep can show you a true thing, a place on the map you hadn’t found, or it can show you a dark one. Where you sleep counts for most, and a lawful town gives clearer nights than a frontier one. But a sleeper who has spent their life looking sees further. Enlightenment and Knowledge make a true dream likelier; Unlife and Chance make a dark one likelier.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	Neither effect is large, and they aren’t meant to be. A virtue at full stretch moves the patron’s roll two steps and the dream’s odds by a few points in a hundred. That’s small, but in Zangband it was nothing.

T	h2	Where this came from
T	h2	\n
T	p0	Topi Ylinen wrote the virtue system for Zangband in 1998, as an Ultima IV-style measure of how a character behaves. It was never finished. By the time development stopped in 2005 there were 168 places in the source that 
T	p0 em	wrote
T	p0	 a virtue and not one that read a virtue back, and the knowledge-screen entry that would have shown them to the player was commented out with the note 
T	p0 em	“Display virtues option is always left out”
T	p0	. Every character in Zangband had eight counters that moved with everything they did, and in seven years not one line of code ever looked at them.
T	p0	\n
T	p0	The selection is Zangband’s, near enough unchanged, because that part already worked. The two things that read a virtue are new, and so is the rule that every virtue a character can be given must be one their play can move. The original had no such rule and wouldn’t have needed one, since nothing was reading.
