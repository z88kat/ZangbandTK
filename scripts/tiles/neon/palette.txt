# palette.txt -- the colours of the Neon tileset.
#
# Every tile is one silhouette drawn in exactly one non-black colour, so a
# tile's colour is data rather than art: it is looked up from the monster's
# own `color:` field, or the terrain's `graphics:` field, in lib/gamedata.
# That is information the other tilesets throw away -- once a graphic tile is
# assigned, ui-map.c:258 short-circuits on `da & 0x80` and the game's own
# colour never reaches the screen.
#
# Ten hues, four tones each, giving forty rows in the tile sheet.  The tones
# are flat colours and not dithers: a 50% dither of a dark hue loses half the
# pixels of a one-pixel limb, which at sixteen pixels is the whole silhouette.
#
# Four and not three because terrain needs a tone for identity *and* a tone
# for the light.  With three, grass (a dark green) and trees (a light green)
# both landed on the same lit colour and the wilderness became one flat field
# -- visible immediately in the preview and invisible in any assertion.
#
# Influence: Jeff Minter / Llamasoft.  Hyper-saturated, and legible because
# each tile only ever carries one of these at a time.

# hue:<name>:<pale>:<neon>:<dim>:<shadow>
#
# The order is the cycle order.  A shimmering monster walks along this list a
# hue at a time, so it has to read as a spectrum -- and grey has to be last,
# because a rainbow that passes through grey looks like the colour dropping
# out rather than moving on.  list.txt's `cycle:` says how many of these are
# cyclable; grey sits outside that count and a grey monster does not shimmer,
# which is honest: there is no grey rainbow.
hue:red:FF9595:FF2B2B:BF1C1C:690F0F
hue:orange:FFC48F:FF8A1F:A85A14:5C310B

# cbhue:<name>:<pale>:<neon>:<dim>:<shadow>
#
# The same ten hues for a colour-blind player: "Neon tiles, colour-blind safe"
# in the tile sets, a second sheet with the same layout, so graf-neo.prf
# serves both.  Every attr line below maps onto these just as it maps onto
# the hues above.
#
# Not chosen by eye, and not by hand.  The measure is what colour blindness
# costs on a map of tiles: two monsters of the same kind -- the same
# silhouette -- told apart only by their colour, a fire hound from a cold one.
# 3,312 such pairs of colours.  Under the Machado (2009) simulation, Neon above
# loses 246 of them to deuteranopia, 172 to protanopia and 56 to tritanopia.
# These lose 7, 6 and 24.  The pairs the game gives a meaning to -- hit points
# green to yellow to red, good and bad effects, a monster's health -- stay at
# least 12 CIEDE2000 apart under all three; in Neon they fall to 6.9.
#
# Found by scripts/tiles/neon-colourblind.py, which searches in OKLCH under
# rules that keep it Neon: each hue near its own angle (green may lean to
# blue-green, which is what parts it from red), the hues still in spectrum
# order for the shimmer to walk, each tone near Neon's lightness and at least
# 70% of its colour, a colour's tones as far apart as Neon's, grey grey,
# every slot over the same contrast floor as Neon's, and nothing normal
# vision could tell apart lost.  Run it with no arguments for the numbers.
#
# What it gives up: red leans to crimson, orange to amber and green to
# aquamarine, which is the price of keeping them apart for a dichromat.  The
# losses left are mostly Light Blue against Light Green under tritanopia --
# leaning green towards blue to save the common forms costs the rare one
# that -- and a few pairs that can only be parted by lightness, where the
# tones already are.
cbhue:red:FF7388:E73B62:B20D41:62212C
cbhue:orange:ECC78A:E5A621:8F6712:583D05
cbhue:yellow:FFFB8E:FAF125:948E15:524F09
cbhue:green:ABFADE:18FBC6:52C8A4:03644D
cbhue:teal:67BDAD:1B9E8C:02695C:003A32
cbhue:cyan:BDF5FE:67EBFD:15B0C1:094C54
cbhue:blue:6C97FC:2D62FC:0A02E1:010557

# attr:<game colour code>:<hue>:<tone>
#
# Chosen by hue family and then by lightness, never by taste.  Angband's
# colours are load-bearing -- white is cold, red burns -- and a player reads
# the colour before the shape.  Three pairs collapse, and each is a pair the
# eye already struggles with in ASCII: Slate/Light Slate, Dark/Light Dark,
# Mustard/Mud.  The other twenty-one survive intact.

# lighting:<prf lighting keyword>:<tone offset>
#
# Terrain keeps its own tone for identity and the light shifts it: a step
# darker when only remembered, a step brighter under a torch right beside you.
# This is the ZX Spectrum BRIGHT bit doing the job it always did -- one
# drawing, three states, no extra art -- and it is the one knob worth turning
# if the walls shout louder than the monsters.  Positive is darker.
lighting:dark:+1
lighting:lit:0
lighting:los:0
lighting:torch:-1

# trap-lighting:<keyword>:<tone offset>
#
# Traps get a shallower ramp than terrain.  A trap you have found is a thing
# to spot, not ground to walk over, so a visible one sits at its own colour
# and only a remembered one dims.
trap-lighting:dark:+1
trap-lighting:lit:0
trap-lighting:los:0
trap-lighting:torch:0
